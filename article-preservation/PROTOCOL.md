# Can whole-article selection preserve useful context?

Sentence compression in the preceding study removed support spans even when both articles still appeared in the prompt. This new check retains selected articles whole, and tests whether a confidence-dependent article count saves reader tokens while preserving short-answer quality. Use a distinct prospective evaluation from the same source, rather than treating it as an independent corpus.

Reuse the frozen upstream reveal-bridge policies, Qwen3-1.7B checkpoint, prompt and decoding from the sentence study. Group the source by support titles as before. Discard the first 2,016 groups used in earlier evaluations, fitting and development. The next 900 train a regularized support-article scorer, the next 160 select its operating point, and the next 192 are evaluated once. Inputs to the supervised article scorer include per-article aggregates of the previously frozen sentence scores and title relevance. Gold article annotations are used for fitting, development selection and final evaluation metrics. They never enter the corresponding held-out inference features. All reader inputs derive from the same originally acquired articles. Count real Qwen prompt token IDs. No extra model or retrieval costs are saved upstream.

Compare full retrieved articles, a fixed rank-depth policy selected from two, three, four, five, six or ten articles, and an adaptive policy on the same ranking. The adaptive policy retains at least two ranked articles and adds further high-scoring articles until the estimated support-score mass of omitted articles is below a common threshold. Test thresholds 0, 0.015, 0.03, 0.06, 0.12, 0.18, 0.24, 0.30, and 1.00. Select the least expensive policy from each family whose complete-support count on development is at least 95% of full context's count. Equal costs favor higher support then the smaller cut parameter. All choices use only relevance annotations and token counts, with no answer generation. The retained articles appear in the same source order and use the same formatting.

The primary contrast is adaptive whole-article selection against full retrieved context. A credible compression result requires at least 30% mean reader input token reduction and the lower paired 95% endpoint of the answer F1 contrast above −4 points. Show exact match, full-support recall and gain against the strong development-selected fixed policy, whether favorable or adverse. The paired bootstrap resamples the 192 support groups. Treat any component analysis as descriptive, and make no claim about latency, other readers or open-corpus retrieval.

The first development-only grid had no eligible setting in one policy family. Before freezing choices, the grid was extended to add more conservative operating points and explicit full-context fallbacks. No inference or test scoring preceded the change.
