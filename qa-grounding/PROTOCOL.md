# Supporting-sentence supervision for a fixed-budget Qwen reader

Previous experiments recovered more supporting articles without a reliable answer gain. Removing sentences also sacrificed accuracy. Here training aims to make a small reader use the same acquired evidence better. Annotated sentence endpoints supervise a training-only head. Evaluation uses the unchanged acquired context, model architecture for decoding, and exact prompt IDs for every arm.

Use the first 640 unused HotpotQA support-title components after the 3,268 groups used by earlier studies to train. The next 64 select checkpoints, followed by 320 untouched groups for final evaluation. Within each component, use one salted, frozen representative. Every input derives from the original reveal-bridge article acquisition at the shared 320-unit cap. The question precedes the passages to make sentence endpoint states question-conditional. Every arm receives the same prompt, title order, and token IDs, at most 1,024 input tokens per case. Keep complete sentence segments when enforcing that cap. The output cap is 24 tokens with a first-line short-answer scorer. The model processes one sequence at a time. No article, answer, or support annotation is supplied at inference beyond the text already selected by frozen retrieval.

Compare frozen Qwen3-1.7B, answer-only QLoRA, and support-supervised QLoRA on the same quantized base checkpoint. In the two trainable arms, use LoRA rank eight and scale two on query, value and MLP-down projections of the final four blocks. Use identical initial LoRA weights, the same ordered 640 examples, batch accumulation of four, 160 AdamW updates at 0.0004, global gradient clipping at one, and identical answer-target likelihood with an EOS. The support-supervised arm also predicts each sentence's supporting-fact label from its causal endpoint with a scalar linear head and a 0.35-weighted class-balanced logistic loss. The answer-only arm receives no sentence loss. Discard the head at inference. It neither generates citations nor gives the reader gold sentence positions at test time. Both trainable arms can independently choose the 80- or 160-update checkpoint by lower mean per-case answer NLL on the 64 development groups. Development uses the same token limit and no final-set feedback. The earlier unused groups are disjoint by normalized gold article title.

The primary estimand is mean paired short-answer F1 difference of support-supervised minus answer-only QLoRA. A practically meaningful win requires at least +3 F1 points and positive paired percentile 95% lower endpoint. Report both adapters against the frozen checkpoint, exact match, answer generation length, and count of wins, losses and ties. The same exact input tokens and output budget apply per test question. Early EOS can change the realized number of generated tokens. Cases are sampled by support groups, not by the official question sampling distribution. Uncertainty across cases conditions on the frozen training seed and checkpoint. No post-hoc selection on final outcomes.

The study is motivated by evidence supervision in [HotpotQA](https://aclanthology.org/D18-1259/) and supervised support detection in [A Simple Yet Strong Pipeline](https://aclanthology.org/2020.emnlp-main.711/). [MARC](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2026/hash/16049e0c3f47899091ac46f8b3afb178-Abstract-Conference.html) motivates training compact readers to use selected evidence. [QLoRA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14314) gives the parameter-efficient quantized adaptation approach. This experiment targets supervised reading of acquired text.
